## Platform

web

## Stack

delegated: recommend the simplest production-ready static marketing stack that supports a polished branded page, hosted application forms, accessibility, and low-cost deployment. ## Users

- First-year Bocconi students looking for a practical head start, context, and a trusted peer one year ahead.
- Second-year Bocconi students who want to mentor a first year and pass on what they have learned.
- The wider Bocconi student community discovering X-Peer through social channels and the landing page.

## Product Purpose

X-Peer is a peer mentorship community at Bocconi, built by Bocconi students for Bocconi students. The first landing page should make the programme legible, credible, and easy to apply to as a mentee or mentor. First-release success is measured primarily by qualified applications, with community trust and recognition supporting that action.

## Positioning

X-Peer replaces the luck of who a student happens to meet in their first weeks with a close, one-year-ahead peer relationship and a wider circle of people to grow with. The programme is intentionally near enough to remember the beginning and far enough ahead to help someone move through it.

## Operating Context

- Visitors will often arrive from Instagram and other social pages on a phone.
- The landing page is the public access point; application review can happen outside the site.
- Applications are expected to be collected with a hosted form and reviewed in Google Sheets or an equivalent shared workspace.
- The first cohort is open for applications; the exact deadlines, selection process, and follow-up communications are still open decisions.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- One responsive landing page with two distinct application paths: first-year mentee and second-year mentor.
- The page must carry the supplied copy structure, Figtree font files, X-Peer logo variants, and four supplied topic icons.
- No custom application backend is required for the first release.
- Hosted forms should support a free or near-zero-cost launch, practical review workflow, and a branded presentation where possible.
- The exact application questions, consent language, storage/retention policy, analytics, social URLs, and deployment/domain are open decisions.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: X-Peer / xpeer.
- Voice: direct, warm, candid, ambitious without hype; lowercase editorial copy is part of the supplied landing copy.
- Typeface: Figtree, supplied locally under `assets/Font - Figtree/`.
- Core palette evidenced by the supplied logo system: forest `#0E4034`, notte `#06231C`, lime `#CDEE5C`, olive `#6B8F14`, and ivory `#F5F4EC`.
- Supplied identity assets in `assets/xpeer-logo/` must be reused rather than redrawn.
- Supplied topic icons cover direction, studies, getting in, and life in Milan.
- The attached mentor social design is a reference for the visual language: forest field, lime emphasis, ivory text, generous type, and profile-led storytelling.

## Evidence on Hand

- `landing-copy.md` contains the approved page narrative, section structure, CTA labels, and positioning copy.
- `assets/` contains the Figtree font family, X-Peer logo/mark/favicon variants, and four topic icon PNGs.
- The attached Instagram mentor card provides a visual reference for social/mentor storytelling.
- No real mentor directory, testimonials, photography set, application questions, or social URLs are currently present in the repository; these must not be invented as factual claims.

## Product Principles

1. Make the first useful relationship feel close, specific, and human.
2. Turn social discovery into a clear next step without forcing visitors through a custom account flow.
3. Show the wider circle as a source of opportunity, not as a generic student club.
4. Keep the public site lightweight; put application operations in tools the founders can actually review.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Open decision: confirm the target standard before implementation. The build should still include keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, reduced-motion support, semantic headings, and clear external-form handoffs by default.
