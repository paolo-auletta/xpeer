## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored, but not yet fully ownable.** The forest/ivory/lime system, Figtree hierarchy, photo-led composition, and desktop mentee-to-mentor route create a coherent X-Peer world. The single lime handoff path is the strongest product-specific device.

Remove the logo and handoff labels, however, and the stock photography plus opening proverb could sell almost any ambitious student community. The page becomes specific again around one year ahead, the four mentorship areas, and the paired audience paths. Those proprietary ideas should be the dominant memory.

**Deterministic scan:** the CLI scan of `src/App.tsx` returned `[]` with exit 0. Browser injection found five anti-pattern groups at desktop and five at mobile. Four categories are likely false positives because they match explicit system rules: the floating-nav border/shadow, Soft Ivory secondary copy on Forest (measured at 7.95:1), and tight leading on large editorial statements. The credible detector issue is the mobile `apply now` label at 10.72px in `src/components/HeaderHero.tsx:47`.

**Visual overlays:** injection succeeded and created five overlay/label pairs, but the T3 tab could not be made reliably user-visible during the assessment and native snapshots failed. No reliable user-facing overlay should be assumed. The fallback evidence was detector serialization, DOM geometry, computed styles, and a separate visual capture for Assessment A.

## Overall Impression

The landing page has a real point of view and an unusually coherent editorial rhythm for a student programme. Its largest weakness is not visual polish but credibility at the moment of commitment: it declares applications open, then silently stops. The single biggest opportunity is to make the one-year-ahead model—not a generic proverb—the first, clearest, and final proof.

## What’s Working

- **The desktop handoff is memorable and specific.** Numbered mentee/mentor labels joined by one lime route explain the relationship without a generic network diagram.
- **The narrative sequence persuades naturally.** Problem → model → practical pathways → wider circle → audience fit → action requires little mental reconstruction.
- **The visual system is disciplined.** Figtree, decisive room colors, restrained lime, flat panels, and recurring circular markers feel authored rather than assembled.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The application endpoint is nonfunctional

**Why it matters:** The page says the first cohort is open, but both terminal `<button>` elements have no handler or link. This blocks the landing page’s only success metric and ends the emotional arc with doubt.

**Fix:** Connect each route to its real hosted form. Until the URLs exist, replace the open claim with an honest state and use a clearly non-interactive coming-soon treatment or a legitimate interest-capture path.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] The page asks for trust without explaining the programme mechanics

**Why it matters:** The four themes explain what students might discuss, but not what follows an application, how matching works, the commitment expected, or when applicants hear back. Aspiration substitutes for reassurance precisely where risk rises.

**Fix:** Once the founders confirm the facts, add a compact how-it-works sequence and a specific post-application expectation. Do not invent unresolved deadlines, policies, or commitments.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The most prominent message is the least ownable

**Why it matters:** The proverb leads the hero and returns in the footer, while the distinctive one-year-ahead proposition is secondary. Stock photography compounds the category-interchangeability.

**Fix:** Lead with an X-Peer-specific promise about a trusted peer one year ahead; keep the proverb only as supporting texture if it earns its place. Replace stock imagery with real X-Peer members when assets exist.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable bolder`

### [P2] Mobile removes the best storytelling device

**Why it matters:** Below 42rem, the mentee/mentor labels, lime route, and one-year-apart caption disappear. The phone-first Instagram audience gets a more generic photo-plus-copy hero.

**Fix:** Preserve a compact handoff marker on mobile or make the one-year relationship unmistakable in the foreground composition without compromising readability.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P2] The mobile navigation CTA label is undersized

**Why it matters:** The `apply now` text computes to 10.72px. Its 98.2×40.8px parent is a usable hit target, but the label itself falls below the detector’s functional-text floor and feels overly compressed.

**Fix:** Raise the mobile label from `0.67rem` to at least `0.6875rem`, preferably `0.72–0.75rem` if the capsule still fits.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`

## Cognitive Load and Emotional Journey

**Cognitive load: low — 0/8 checklist failures.** The page has one clear focus, sensible chunking and grouping, strong hierarchy, no decision point above four visible options, no working-memory bridge, and a linear disclosure sequence. The problem is not overload; it is insufficient decision confidence.

The emotional arc is structurally sound: aspiration → the luck problem → a one-year-ahead answer → wider community → audience fit → action. The valley arrives immediately before commitment because the programme’s mechanics remain unknown. The dead application buttons then break the peak-end rule: urgency is raised, but momentum has nowhere to go.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan — first-timer:** Quickly understands mentee versus mentor, but cannot tell what happens after applying, how matching works, or what commitment is expected. The final buttons provide neither action nor explanation.
- **Riley — stress tester:** Repeated hero and audience CTAs all funnel to a terminal state that silently fails. “Applications are open” contradicts the implementation; aspirational outcomes are not distinguished from established evidence.
- **Casey — distracted mobile user:** The one-year handoff disappears from the mobile hero, slowing comprehension during a short social-media visit. The header’s functional CTA label is also unusually small.
- **First-year Bocconi student arriving from Instagram:** The four topics speak to real concerns, but the generic opening delays the practical head-start value. Missing process and follow-up expectations weaken confidence.
- **Second-year mentor candidate:** The emotional reason to mentor is clear, but responsibilities, cadence, matching, and selection remain undefined. “Join as a mentor” ends at a dead button.

## Minor Observations

- Mobile section navigation disappears entirely; only Apply remains.
- CTA terminology is understandable but not fully systematic.
- First-year students and second-year students are visually major card titles but are paragraphs rather than headings.
- Stock-photo alt text is generic and accurate, but the imagery should not be mistaken for actual X-Peer members.
- The opening and footer spend two premium narrative positions repeating the same generic proverb.
- One Assessment A fallback capture appeared to show right-edge clipping at 390px, but Assessment B measured no horizontal overflow and all early actions within the viewport. Source geometry supports B, so clipping is not treated as a confirmed issue; it merits a manual device check rather than a fix ticket.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the sentence visitors remember be a proverb anyone can use, or a claim only X-Peer can make?
- If the forms are not ready, would an honest register-interest state build more trust than declaring applications open?
- What single factual commitment—matching method, cadence, or response timing—would prove this is a real programme rather than another student association?
- On mobile, what is the smallest visual that still makes one year apart unmistakable?
